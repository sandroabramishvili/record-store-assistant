---
title: Original Pressing vs. Reissue
topic: pressings
---
An **original pressing** is a copy made when the album was first released, from the first production run in the album's home country. A **reissue** is any later pressing: sometimes a few years later, sometimes decades later, often by a different company.

**Why do people pay more for originals?**

- **History:** an original is an object from the moment the music first came out, with the period label, cover printing and inserts.
- **Sound:** many originals were cut from the original master tapes when those tapes were fresh. Some later reissues were made from copies of copies or from digital files, and can sound flatter.
- **Scarcity:** original jazz and rock pressings from the 1950s to the 1970s exist in limited numbers, and good copies get rarer every year.

**Why a reissue is often the smarter buy:**

- **Price:** a modern reissue usually costs a fraction of an original. Some sought-after originals cost hundreds or even thousands of dollars.
- **Condition:** a new reissue is clean and quiet. An affordable original is often VG or worse, with surface noise.
- **Quality reissue series:** several modern series are cut carefully from the original analog tapes, pressed on quiet vinyl and packaged with great care. Many listeners cannot hear a difference from an original, and some prefer them.

**So which should you buy?** If you want to listen to the music in great sound at a fair price, a good modern reissue is hard to beat. If you love the history and the object, or want an investment, look for an original in the best condition you can afford. Our listings always show both years: the year of the pressing and the year the album first came out.
