---
title: Media vs. Sleeve: How to Read a Record Listing
topic: grading
---
A listing such as "VG+ / VG" means the vinyl is VG+ and the cover is VG. The two are graded separately because they age differently: a record can sound wonderful in a worn cover, and a perfect-looking cover can hide a scratched disc.

**Which grade matters more?** If you buy records to listen to them, the media grade matters most. If you collect records as objects, or plan to resell, the sleeve matters too: an original cover in great shape can be worth as much as the record.

**Common sleeve terms you will see in our notes:**

- **Ring wear:** a faint circle on the cover where the record pressed against it over the years. Very common on older records and mostly cosmetic.
- **Seam split:** the cover's glued edge has come apart, often along the bottom or the spine. Small splits are normal for records from the 1960s and 1970s.
- **Writing or stickers:** a name, a price tag or a radio-station stamp. These lower the sleeve grade but tell a story of their own.
- **Gatefold:** a cover that opens like a book. Check the hinge, which is where wear shows first.

**Common media terms:**

- **Plays through / plays perfectly:** our staff played it and found no skips, even if it shows marks.
- **Light surface noise:** a soft background hiss or occasional tick, typical of VG copies.
- **Warp:** the record is not perfectly flat. A slight dish warp usually plays fine; a strong warp can make the needle jump.

The "notes" field on each copy in our catalog tells you anything we noticed that the grade alone does not capture.
