---
title: Where to Start with Jazz on Vinyl
topic: genre
---
Jazz and the LP grew up together: in the late 1950s and 1960s, labels such as Blue Note, Prestige, Impulse! and Columbia made some of the best-sounding records ever pressed. That is why jazz is one of the most rewarding genres to collect on vinyl.

**Start with the calm classics.** Modal jazz is spacious and melodic, a perfect entry point. Miles Davis's *Kind of Blue* is the most famous example and the record many people start with.

**Then add some energy: hard bop.** Hard bop is bluesier, punchier and full of catchy themes. Look at Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers' *Moanin'*, Lee Morgan's *The Sidewinder* or Cannonball Adderley's *Somethin' Else*. If you liked *Kind of Blue* but want something more upbeat, this is where to go.

**Go deeper: spiritual and modal jazz.** John Coltrane's *A Love Supreme* is intense and devotional. Modern artists such as Kamasi Washington carry this tradition forward on big, cinematic records.

**Cross over: jazz-funk and fusion.** In the 1970s, jazz musicians embraced funk grooves and synthesizers. Herbie Hancock's *Head Hunters* and Donald Byrd's *Black Byrd* are fun, danceable and often more affordable than 1950s originals.

**Collector's tip:** original Blue Note pressings from the 1950s and 1960s are very expensive. Excellent all-analog reissue series exist, and they are a great way to build a jazz shelf without spending a fortune.
