---
title: A Beginner's Guide to Soul and Funk Records
topic: genre
---
Soul and funk are made for vinyl: warm voices, live bands and deep bass lines sound wonderful on a good pressing, and many classic albums are still affordable.

**Classic soul (1960s).** Gospel-trained singers with powerful bands. Aretha Franklin's *Lady Soul* shows soul at its most commanding. Labels to look out for include Atlantic, Stax and Motown.

**Soul grows up (early 1970s).** Artists began making whole albums with a message. Marvin Gaye's *What's Going On* and Curtis Mayfield's *Super Fly* are song cycles about society, wrapped in lush arrangements. Stevie Wonder's *Songs in the Key of Life* is a joyful double album full of hits.

**Funk.** Funk puts the groove first: tight rhythm guitar, syncopated bass and horns. Parliament's *Mothership Connection* is a perfect party record. Afrobeat, pioneered by Fela Kuti, mixes funk with West African rhythms into long, hypnotic tracks.

**Jazz-funk.** Where funk meets jazz improvisation: Herbie Hancock, Donald Byrd and Roy Ayers made sunny, sample-friendly records that hip-hop producers later loved.

**What to check when buying:** soul and funk records were often played at parties, so used copies vary a lot. Look at the media grade carefully and ask about surface noise. A VG+ copy of a big-selling 1970s album is usually easy to find and reasonably priced; rare private-press funk records can be expensive.

**Follow the samples:** if you love hip-hop, many of its classic beats were built from these records. Digging into the originals is one of the great joys of collecting.
