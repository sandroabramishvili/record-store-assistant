---
title: Reading Matrix and Runout Numbers
topic: pressings
---
Look closely at the smooth band of vinyl between the last song and the label. This is the **runout groove** (also called the dead wax). Most records have letters and numbers scratched or stamped there. Collectors call them the **matrix** or **runout** numbers, and they are the best way to identify exactly which pressing you are holding.

**What you can find there:**

- **The matrix number:** a catalog-style code for each side, often ending in a letter or number that changes with every new lacquer or stamper. A low suffix usually points to an early pressing.
- **Mastering engineer marks:** many engineers signed their work with initials or a nickname. Collectors look for certain names because those cuts are known for great sound.
- **Plant marks:** small symbols or letters that show which factory pressed the record. The same album was often pressed in several plants at once.
- **Messages:** some artists and engineers scratched jokes or short messages into the runout.

**Why it matters:** two copies with identical covers and labels can come from different years or factories. The runout tells you which one you have, which affects both value and, sometimes, sound.

**How to read it:** hold the record at an angle under a bright light and turn it slowly. Write down everything you see on both sides, then compare it with a database such as Discogs, where collectors list the runout details of known pressings.
