---
title: How to Store Records
topic: care
---
Good storage keeps records flat, clean and playable for decades. Bad storage can ruin a valuable record in one summer.

**Always store records upright.** Stand them vertically, like books on a shelf, packed firmly enough that they do not lean but not so tight that you have to force them out. Records stacked flat on top of each other carry weight on the bottom discs and can warp or develop ring wear on the covers.

**Keep them cool and dry.** Heat is the biggest enemy: a record left in a hot car or next to a radiator can warp within hours. Aim for normal room temperature, away from direct sunlight, heaters and damp walls. Very humid rooms can cause mould on covers and paper sleeves.

**Use good sleeves.**

- **Inner sleeves:** anti-static, poly-lined inner sleeves protect the vinyl better than plain paper, which sheds fibres and can leave hairline scratches. Keep the original printed inner sleeve with the record if it has artwork or lyrics.
- **Outer sleeves:** a clear plastic outer sleeve protects the cover from shelf wear, ring wear and spills.

**Handle with care.** Hold records by the edges and the label, never on the playing surface. Fingerprints leave oils that attract dust.

**Sturdy shelves.** A full shelf of LPs is very heavy, roughly 15 to 20 kilograms per 100 records. Use furniture designed for that weight.

Store records like this and the copy you buy today will still play beautifully for the next owner.
