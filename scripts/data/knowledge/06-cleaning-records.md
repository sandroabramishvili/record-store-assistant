---
title: How to Clean Your Records
topic: care
---
Dust and old grime in the grooves cause much of the crackle people blame on vinyl. Cleaning a record properly can lift its sound noticeably, sometimes by a whole grade.

**Before every play:** give the record a light sweep with a **carbon-fibre brush** while it spins on the turntable. Hold the brush still for a few rotations, then slowly angle it off the record to lift the dust away. This takes ten seconds and prevents dust from being pressed into the grooves.

**For a deeper clean (used records, or anything noisy):**

1. Use a dedicated **record cleaning fluid** or a mix of distilled water with a small amount of record-safe surfactant. Do not use household cleaners, alcohol-heavy mixtures on shellac, or tap water, which leaves mineral spots.
2. Apply the fluid with a soft microfibre pad or goat-hair brush, moving **along the grooves** in a circle, never across them.
3. Keep the label dry. A label protector or a simple cleaning clamp helps.
4. Dry the record with a clean microfibre cloth, again following the grooves, or let it air-dry standing upright.

**Cleaning machines:** vacuum cleaning machines and ultrasonic cleaners give the best results and are worth it if you buy a lot of used records. Many shops, including ours, can clean a record for you.

**Do not forget the stylus:** a dirty needle drags dirt back into clean grooves. Clean it gently with a stylus brush from back to front.

After cleaning, put the record into a **fresh inner sleeve**: old paper sleeves shed fibres and can scratch.
