---
title: The Goldmine Grading Scale
topic: grading
---
Every used record in our shop carries two grades: one for the vinyl itself (the media) and one for the cover (the sleeve). We use the Goldmine scale, the most common standard among record dealers. From best to worst:

**M (Mint).** Perfect in every way, usually still sealed. We rarely use this grade for anything that has been opened.

**NM (Near Mint).** Looks and plays as if it was just bought. No visible marks except perhaps a tiny spot under strong light. No surface noise beyond what the pressing itself has.

**VG+ (Very Good Plus).** A record that was played and cared for. You may see light marks or faint scuffs when you tilt it under a lamp, but they do not affect the sound in any meaningful way. During the quietest passages you might hear an occasional soft tick, but no constant crackle. For most listeners, VG+ is the sweet spot between price and sound quality.

**VG (Very Good).** Clearly played. Light scratches you can see, and some surface noise you will hear, especially in quiet parts and between tracks. The music still comes through well, and the price is usually much lower than VG+.

**G+ / G (Good Plus / Good).** Plays all the way through without skipping, but with constant surface noise, crackle and possibly some distortion. Good for a "placeholder" copy of something rare or for sampling.

**F / P (Fair / Poor).** Skips, heavy damage, often barely playable. We do not usually sell these except as collectibles or wall art.

**Will a VG+ record crackle?** Usually not in a way you notice while the music plays. Expect a quiet tick or two in silent moments, not a constant crackle. If you are very sensitive to surface noise, choose NM or a new pressing; if you want the best value, VG+ is the classic choice.

Grading is done by eye and by listening, so it is partly subjective. Our staff play-test any record graded VG+ or lower when the visual grade is unclear, and we note anything unusual on the listing.
