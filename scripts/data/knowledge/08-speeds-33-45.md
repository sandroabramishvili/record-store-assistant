---
title: 33⅓ vs. 45 RPM: Record Speeds Explained
topic: equipment
---
Records are made to spin at a set speed, measured in revolutions per minute (RPM). Playing a record at the wrong speed makes it sound too slow and deep, or too fast and high-pitched.

**33⅓ RPM** is the standard speed for **LPs** (long-playing albums), usually 12-inch records with about 18 to 25 minutes of music per side. Almost every album in our catalog plays at 33⅓.

**45 RPM** is the classic speed for **7-inch singles**, with one song per side. Many **12-inch singles**, such as dance and club records, also play at 45, because the faster speed gives wider grooves and more volume and bass. Some audiophile reissues spread an album over two discs at 45 RPM for the same reason.

**78 RPM** was the speed of old shellac records from the first half of the twentieth century. They need a special stylus and most modern turntables cannot play them.

**How to tell the speed:** look at the label or the cover; it is usually printed there. If not, a 7-inch with a large centre hole is almost always 45; a 12-inch album is almost always 33⅓; a 12-inch single may be either, so check the label.

**Why faster can sound better:** at 45 RPM the needle travels through more groove per second, so there is more room for detail and dynamics, at the cost of shorter playing time.

Most turntables switch speed with a button or by moving the belt on the motor pulley. If a familiar song sounds strangely slow or chipmunk-fast, check the speed first.
