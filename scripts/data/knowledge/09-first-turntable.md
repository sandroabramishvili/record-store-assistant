---
title: Choosing Your First Turntable
topic: equipment
---
A good first turntable does not need to be expensive, but a few choices make a big difference to how your records sound and how long they last.

**Avoid the cheapest all-in-one suitcase players.** Many very cheap players have heavy, poorly adjusted tonearms and basic ceramic cartridges that can wear records faster. They are fine as decoration, but not for records you care about.

**What to look for:**

- **Adjustable counterweight and anti-skate:** these let you set the tracking force correctly, which protects your records and improves sound.
- **A replaceable cartridge:** the cartridge (with its stylus) is the part that reads the groove. Being able to upgrade or replace it extends the life of your turntable.
- **Belt drive or direct drive:** both work well. Belt drive isolates motor noise; direct drive starts quickly and holds speed steadily, which DJs like.
- **Built-in phono preamp (optional):** a turntable's signal is very quiet and needs a phono stage. Some turntables include one; otherwise your amplifier needs a "phono" input, or you add a separate phono preamp.

**The rest of the chain:** a turntable needs an amplifier and speakers, or powered speakers with a phono input. Good speakers often improve the sound more than an expensive turntable.

**Set it up well:** place the turntable on a level, stable surface away from the speakers, set the tracking force as the cartridge maker recommends, and replace the stylus when it wears out (often after several hundred to around a thousand hours of play).
